## Composability

The change introduced a coupling in `delivery/__init__.py` that the registry pattern was meant to avoid. The file now explicitly imports `acme, beacon` to trigger their registration side effects. Adding a third provider will require editing this file again, defeating the "add a module and register it" promise in `delivery/registry.py`. The `app.py` change is benign — it simply threads a provider name through — but the delivery boundary now knows all concrete providers by name.

## Domain alignment

Concepts remain well-located. `Notification` in `notifications/model.py` stays pure (user_id, channel, subject, body — no provider awareness). Provider-specific concerns (endpoints, headers, payload shapes, auth schemes) are confined to `delivery/providers/acme.py` and the new `delivery/providers/beacon.py`. The retry policy and outcome vocabulary live in each provider handler, duplicated but not shared — acceptable since the contract forbids changing them. The registry owns the name→handler mapping; `delivery/__init__.py` acts as a thin dispatcher.

## Change locality

Proportionate. Three files touched: `app.py` (thread the parameter), `delivery/__init__.py` (accept parameter, import both providers), and the new `delivery/providers/beacon.py`. The registry, transport seam, and notification model are untouched. The only non-local concern is the import list in `delivery/__init__.py` — a known limitation of the side-effect registration style. If more providers arrive, that file becomes a changelog rather than a stable boundary.
